How to Trade Crude Oil in India

A complete 2026 guide for Indian traders — WTI vs Brent, MCX crude oil, key price drivers, trading hours and a practical step-by-step risk management approach.

Why Crude Oil is Significant for Indian Traders

India is the world's third-largest crude oil consumer and imports over 80% of its oil requirements. This makes crude oil prices uniquely important for Indian investors — it directly affects inflation, the Indian Rupee, the Current Account Deficit, and the profitability of many Indian companies (refining, aviation, logistics, paints).

Beyond its macro importance, crude oil is one of the most actively traded commodities globally, offering significant opportunities for short-term traders who understand its drivers.

WTI vs Brent Crude Oil — What's the Difference?

  • WTI (West Texas Intermediate): The US crude oil benchmark, traded on NYMEX (CME Group). Priced in USD per barrel. This is the most actively traded oil futures contract globally.
  • Brent Crude: The international benchmark, covering North Sea oil. Used to price approximately two-thirds of the world's internationally traded oil. Slightly heavier geopolitical premium than WTI.
  • MCX Crude Oil (India): The Multi Commodity Exchange of India (MCX) offers crude oil futures denominated in INR. This is the most accessible route for Indian retail traders — no foreign remittance needed, regulated by SEBI/FMC.
For most Indian retail traders: MCX Crude Oil futures are the easiest starting point — they are regulated, INR-denominated and do not require LRS remittance or international broker accounts.

Crude Oil Trading Hours (IST)

  • MCX Crude Oil: 9:00 AM – 11:30 PM IST (Monday to Friday)
  • WTI Futures (NYMEX): Nearly 24 hours on weekdays. US regular session peak: ~7:30 PM – 2:00 AM IST
  • Key volatility windows: US EIA Inventory Report — every Wednesday ~8:00 PM IST; OPEC announcements (variable)

Key Drivers of Crude Oil Prices

Understanding what moves oil prices is essential before trading. The main factors are:

  • OPEC+ production decisions: OPEC and allied producers control a large share of global supply. Cut announcements typically push prices higher; production increases put downward pressure.
  • US EIA Weekly Inventory Report: Released every Wednesday. Larger-than-expected inventory builds are bearish; draws are bullish for prices.
  • US Rig Count (Baker Hughes): Released every Friday. Higher rig counts signal rising US production, which is bearish for prices.
  • Geopolitical events: Conflicts or instability in oil-producing regions (Middle East, Russia) typically spike oil prices due to supply risk.
  • Global demand outlook: Economic growth data from China, US and Europe heavily influences demand expectations and oil prices.
  • USD strength: Oil is priced in USD globally. A stronger US Dollar typically puts downward pressure on oil prices (and vice versa).

How to Trade Crude Oil from India — Access Methods

  • MCX Crude Oil Futures (Recommended for beginners): Open a commodity trading account with a SEBI-registered broker. Trade in lot sizes of 100 barrels (standard) or 10 barrels (mini contracts). Fully regulated, INR-settled.
  • International WTI/Brent Futures: Via international brokers (Interactive Brokers, etc.) funded through the LRS scheme. Provides access to the most liquid global oil contracts.
  • Crude Oil CFDs: Offered by offshore platforms. Flexible lot sizes and leverage, but not SEBI-regulated.
  • Trading Apps: The Tradew App provides Indian users access to commodity and energy products. Verify product availability and terms before trading.

Step-by-Step: How to Start Trading Crude Oil

  1. Open a commodity account — For MCX crude, open a commodity segment account with a SEBI-registered broker (your existing stock broker may already offer this). For international access, use the Tradew App or an international platform.
  2. Complete KYC — Submit PAN, Aadhaar, and bank details. MCX onboarding through Indian brokers is typically the fastest.
  3. Fund your account — Ensure you have sufficient capital for margin requirements. MCX mini crude oil contracts require lower margin than standard contracts.
  4. Track the economic calendar — Note the weekly EIA report every Wednesday (~8:00 PM IST) and OPEC meeting dates. Avoid entering new trades just before these events unless you have a specific strategy.
  5. Enter with a defined stop-loss — Oil can move ₹50–200 per barrel intraday. Set your stop-loss before you enter. Know your maximum acceptable loss per trade.
  6. Monitor and manage — Watch for unexpected news (geopolitical events, surprise inventory data). Have a clear exit rule for both profit targets and stop-losses.

Risk Management for Oil Trading

  • Position sizing is critical: Oil is highly volatile. Limit each trade to a small percentage of your total trading capital (1–2% risk per trade is common practice).
  • Beware news gaps: OPEC announcements and geopolitical events can cause oil prices to gap by several percent. Overnight positions carry higher risk.
  • Rollover dates: MCX and international futures expire monthly. Be aware of contract rollover dates — prices can deviate from spot during expiry week.
  • INR impact on MCX: MCX crude oil is priced in INR but benchmarked to WTI. Currency movements affect MCX pricing independent of global oil moves.
Note: Crude oil trading can generate significant short-term profits, but is equally capable of generating rapid losses. Never trade with money you cannot afford to lose.
